When More Variables Add Less Information

Norwegian industrial forecasting: a controlled test of whether additional economic drivers improve on a disciplined autoregressive baseline.

Research Intelligence Brief

The incremental information test

Central finding. In two prespecified retrospective comparisons, three extra lagged industrial variables did not reduce one-step forecast RMSE relative to AR(2). This is evidence against incremental predictive value in these specifications and this revised-data sample, not evidence that the economic channels are absent.

Pattern: multi-factor RMSE below AR(2) in a genuinely untouched future vintage → consider incremental predictive value. Competing explanations: lag mismatch, revised-data leakage, model instability, weak marginal information, changing sector composition. Required evidence: original publication vintages, nested-model inference, alternative lags and pre-registered holdouts.

What would change our mind? Consistent improvement over AR(2) across independent time blocks and initial-release data, with economically material gains and robust uncertainty estimates.

Decision consequence: retain AR(2) as the provisional operational benchmark; do not increase model complexity on the strength of in-sample fit alone.

Methodology and limitations

Monthly industrial production indices and turnover indices from SSB 07095 and 07096 are converted to twelve-month log differences. Models are estimated by OLS in an expanding training window. Benchmarks: previous year's-growth observation (naive), AR(1), and AR(2). The extended model adds three industrial drivers lagged two months alongside AR(1) and AR(2). Forecasts are generated one month ahead and scored using RMSE and MAE.

Leakage controls: each expanding-window fit excludes its target observation and all later rows; external regressors use a two-month lag. Unresolved: the uploaded SSB exports contain revised history rather than contemporaneous publication vintages. Consequently, this is a pseudo-out-of-sample test and not proof of real-time information availability. Source revisions, reporting calendars and series redefinitions could change the result.

Monthly annual-growth rates overlap and are serially dependent; inference is exploratory. AR(2) is an explicit baseline, not a claim of optimal forecasting architecture. Two target variables share inputs and should not be interpreted as independent replications.

Data provenance: SSB table 07095 (industrial production) and SSB table 07096 (industrial turnover); user exports dated 08 November 2026. Full replication requires the exact downloaded CSVs and the Python scripts used for estimation; this standalone webpage reports the resulting aggregates and does not contain the full input series.
