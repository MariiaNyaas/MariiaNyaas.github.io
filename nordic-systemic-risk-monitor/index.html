Level 02 · mechanisms

Transmission pathways & competing explanations

Structured hypotheses to investigate before interpreting an observed signal as evidence of causation.

Rates → households

Policy and market rates can raise debt-service costs at repricing dates, compress disposable income and increase arrears.

Alternative: income growth, fixed-rate contracts, tax relief and savings buffers may offset the channel.

Property → collateral

Declining real-estate valuations may amplify loss given default and tighten credit availability.

Alternative: conservative initial LTV, amortisation and guarantees may dampen transmission.

Funding → liquidity

Higher wholesale spreads and shorter funding maturities can increase refinancing pressure.

Alternative: stable deposits, central-bank facilities and pre-funded liquidity may absorb shocks.

These are mechanisms to test, not demonstrated causal estimates for any country. Identification requires time-series controls, lags, alternative specifications and independent data.

Methodology, independent evidence & governance

Six-factor vulnerability specification

For each market, transform available indicators into directional risk scores using fixed, explicitly illustrative bounds: unemployment 2–12%, absolute CPI inflation 0–12%, negative GDP growth 0–8%, private credit / GDP 40–200%, NPL 0–15%, capital-to-assets shortfall relative to 12%. Each factor is clipped to 0–100 and the arithmetic mean is reported if at least four of six indicators are present.

Direction and bounds are judgemental; inflation below zero is not symmetrically penalised. A score cannot be compared to a calibrated probability of distress. The denominator changes with coverage; inspect coverage before comparing countries.

Source integrity

Machine-readable observations: World Bank Indicators API. The World Bank consolidates upstream statistical series; this is not six independently verified data providers. Cross-check context: Norges Bank, Riksbank, Danmarks Nationalbank, Bank of Finland, Central Bank of Iceland, and IMF.
