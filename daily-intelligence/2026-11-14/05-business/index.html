Why the AI Pipeline Is Not a Funnel

Current use and “ever considered” are separate survey measures—not conversion stages

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. For enterprises with 10+ employees in 2025, reported AI use is 28.89% in Norway, 42.03% in Denmark, 37.82% in Finland and 35.04% in Sweden.
  2. The corresponding separate “ever considered” measure is 10.80% in Norway, 16.25% in Denmark, 19.47% in Finland and 7.15% in Sweden.
  3. In Norway, current use rises from 25.20% among firms with 10–49 employees to 66.04% among firms with 250+ employees.
  4. Because the two indicators are not established as nested populations, their difference is descriptive and must not be interpreted as a conversion rate.

The mechanism and the test

A dashboard can create a false funnel merely by placing “considered” before “using”. Survey routing, respondent populations and timing can make those percentages non-nested. The economically relevant lesson is measurement discipline: adoption momentum cannot be inferred from a ratio whose denominator relationship is unverified.

Methodology & limitations

Eurostat workbook dataset isoc_eb_ai. Select two exact indicators: enterprises using at least one listed AI technology and enterprises that ever considered using one. Pair only observations with identical geography, year and enterprise-size class. Preserve flags and missing years; no interpolation. Display both levels and their arithmetic difference, but do not call it a pipeline, conversion or adoption probability. Repeated cross-sections; supplied workbook contains no microdata or sampling standard errors. Econometric validation is not applicable to the definitional claim; inferential confidence intervals are NOT VERIFIED.

Separate survey indicators are compared within identical geography, year and size class. This is not a firm-level funnel: respondents and questionnaire routing may differ, and 'ever considered' is not assumed to contain every current user. What Would Change Our Mind

  • Questionnaire routing documentation proving nested populations.
  • Firm-level transitions from consideration to production use.
  • Sampling errors and revision histories supporting uncertainty estimates.
