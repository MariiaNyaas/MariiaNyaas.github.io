The Repayment-Strain Transmission Test

Mortgage rates, reported payment problems and the limits of a small forward test

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. The retained household mortgage-rate series reaches 2026M09 at 5.31%; the annual survey measure ends in 2025.
  2. Reported mortgage-payment problems were 1.6% for all persons in 2025 and 1.7% in the lowest income quartile.
  3. Across 8 expanding one-year origins, the exploratory rate-plus-persistence rule has MAE 0.172 pp versus 0.188 pp for persistence.
  4. The 95% two-year-block interval for mean MAE improvement is -0.094 to 0.128 pp; it crosses zero, so predictive superiority is not established.

The mechanism and the test

Higher loan rates can transmit into household cash-flow strain, but survey payment problems also reflect income, buffers, refinancing and sampling variation. The test asks whether the rate level adds forward information beyond simply carrying the last survey reading forward; it does not estimate default probability.

Methodology & limitations

SSB 10745: monthly rates on outstanding loans secured on dwellings to households, 2013M12–2026M09. SSB 12128: annual percentage reporting problems paying mortgages, 2011–2025, with income-group detail. Average monthly rates by calendar year. At each eligible origin, fit next-year survey percentage on a constant, the current percentage and current annual mortgage rate using only prior rows; compare with persistence. Eight latest-vintage expanding origins. Paired absolute-error improvement uses 4,000 circular block draws, block length two years, seed 20261114. Not a real-time-vintage backtest.

Annual survey percentages are not arrears balances, PD or bank losses. Small latest-vintage sample; survey and rate revisions are not archived in real time. The regression is exploratory and its forecast origins are not independent.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • New untouched annual survey observations with a stable loss advantage.
  • Borrower-level arrears, balances and exposure denominators linking strain to realised credit loss.
  • A result that survives alternative survey definitions, revision vintages and income controls.
