The Capital-Intensity Memory Test

Does smoothing improve the audit-planning baseline?

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. The test contains 16 issuer-year holdouts across six SEC filers; each target uses only earlier annual facts for that issuer.
  2. A trailing three-year median produces MAE 28.19 percentage points versus 22.64 for the last reported ratio.
  3. The candidate is worse by 5.55 pp on average; smoothing does not improve the point estimate.
  4. The issuer-cluster 95% interval for baseline-minus-model loss is -11.57 to 1.46 pp and includes zero.

The mechanism and the test

Audit planning often smooths volatile ratios. But capital-intensity regimes can shift faster than a three-year median. A mechanically smoother series is useful only if it forecasts the next filing better than the last observed ratio.

Methodology & limitations

Six SEC Company Facts exports; USD, form 10-K, filing date no later than 14 November 2026, annual duration 330–380 days, period ends from 2015. CFO uses NetCashProvidedByUsedInOperatingActivities. Cash capex uses PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment with documented fallback to PaymentsToAcquireProductiveAssets. Ratio = cash capex / CFO for positive-CFO years. For the last three eligible years per issuer, forecast with the median of the preceding three ratios and compare with the immediately preceding ratio. Latest-filing vintage, not archived real time. Resample issuers as clusters, 4,000 draws, seed 20261114.

The ratio uses cash capex and CFO, not total economic investment. CFO includes working-capital effects; cash capex excludes some leases and noncash additions. Six issuers and issuer-cluster resampling cannot establish a universal forecasting law. Fiscal years differ.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • More issuer histories and pre-specified industry cohorts.
  • A stable median advantage in new filings and under alternate capex perimeters.
  • Footnote reconciliations showing that noncash investment reverses the measured regime shifts.
