The Fiscal-Balance Forecasting Trap

Component smoothing versus persistence in Norway’s nominal net lending

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. The retained consolidated general-government sample spans 1995–2024 and reconciles revenue minus expenditure to reported net lending within one NOK million in every year.
  2. Across the last ten one-year origins, a five-year-median component rule has MAE NOK 321.80bn versus NOK 300.50bn for persistence.
  3. The component rule is worse by NOK 21.30bn on the point estimate.
  4. The two-year-block 95% interval for baseline-minus-model improvement is NOK -61.39bn to 16.90bn; it crosses zero.

The mechanism and the test

Forecasting revenue and expenditure separately feels more structural than carrying forward the fiscal balance. In a petroleum-sensitive nominal system, however, component medians can miss abrupt revenue regimes and generate false precision. A more detailed model earns credibility only by beating persistence out of sample.

Methodology & limitations

SSB 10721, consolidated general government, nominal NOK million, 1995–2024. Reconcile A total revenue − E total expenditure = F net lending/borrowing. At each origin, forecast revenue and expenditure with their respective median growth rates over the preceding five observed years; subtract to obtain next-year net lending. Compare with prior-year net lending. Report the last ten expanding origins. Paired absolute-error improvement uses 4,000 circular draws, two-year blocks, seed 20261114. Latest-vintage test, not real-time archival validation. Change-variance shares use covariance with Δnet and sum to one algebraically; they are not causal attribution.

Nominal consolidated general-government flows, 1995–2024. This is not the structural non-oil budget balance, fiscal-rule headroom or a sovereign-credit model. The component rule is exploratory and latest-vintage; petroleum prices, inflation and revisions are not modelled. Variance contributions are accounting covariance shares, not causal effects.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • Real-time vintages and a pre-specified petroleum-price model that beats persistence.
  • Structural non-oil balance and inflation-adjusted series.
  • A stable advantage through new revenue regimes rather than only historical refits.
