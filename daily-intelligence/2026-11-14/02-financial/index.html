The Power-Diversification Stress Test

Frozen cross-zone weights versus equal weighting in a held-out half-sample

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. The source contains 8,760 indexed observations for each of five Norwegian bidding zones; clock and DST alignment remain NOT VERIFIED.
  2. Frozen long-only weights produce held-out volatility of 262.24 NOK/MWh versus 265.21 for an equal-weight price index.
  3. Average pairwise correlation is 0.585 in training and 0.592 in the held-out half.
  4. The weekly-block 95% interval for paired variance improvement is -34,964 to 30,610 squared-price units; diversification superiority is not statistically resolved.

The mechanism and the test

Geographic procurement can lower price variability only when zone differences persist out of sample. A covariance-optimised index can look stable in training yet concentrate exposure in one zone. The analysis therefore freezes weights before the held-out half and keeps equal weighting visible.

Methodology & limitations

Read five price columns from the 2025 workbook, retain all 8,760 indexed rows and convert øre/kWh to NOK/MWh by multiplying by ten. First 4,380 rows train the covariance matrix. Add a ridge equal to 10% of its diagonal, solve inverse-covariance weights, clip negative weights to zero and renormalise. Freeze weights for the remaining 4,380 rows. Compare standard deviation with equal weighting and individual zones. Circular 168-observation blocks, 4,000 draws, seed 20261114, evaluate paired squared-deviation improvement. This is price-index validation, not a trading backtest.

The workbook has 8,760 indexed observations but clock time, time zone and DST alignment remain NOT VERIFIED. Price-index volatility is not investment return, dispatch profit or an executable hedge. Long-only weights use a stated ridge and were selected after inspecting this dataset.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • Authenticated timestamps and an untouched subsequent year.
  • A stable advantage across ridge choices, seasons and feasible contract weights.
  • Evidence that grid, settlement or basis costs remove the apparent diversification benefit.
