The Stewardship-Cell Dominance Map

Single-name dependence inside country–industry review cells

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. The 30 June 2026 snapshot contains 7,077 listed-equity rows grouped into 446 country–industry cells.
  2. Requiring at least three holdings leaves 318 analytical cells.
  3. At a 50% top-name threshold, 138 cells qualify and represent 15.18% of exported listed-equity value.
  4. At thresholds of 30%, 50% and 70%, flagged value shares are 31.59%, 15.18% and 7.73%, respectively.

The mechanism and the test

Stewardship resources are often organised by market and sector. Inside such a review cell, one issuer can dominate both attention and value even when the total portfolio is broadly diversified. The map is a workflow diagnostic, not a legal-control or misconduct test.

Methodology & limitations

Read the NBIM 30 June 2026 equity snapshot in UTF-16, semicolon separated. Retain rows with numeric market value, country and industry. Group by reported country × reported industry. Within each cell calculate total value, number of holdings, top issuer share, HHI and effective number of names. Default eligibility requires at least three holdings; dominance requires top issuer share at least 50%. Denominator for value share is all exported listed-equity value. Sensitivity thresholds: 30/40/50/60/70%. Snapshot analysis; forecast backtesting is not applicable.

One 30 June 2026 listed-equity snapshot. A country-industry cell is an analytical grouping, not a legal or voting-control perimeter. Market value is not risk contribution. No return history means backtesting and causal claims are not applicable.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • Issuer-level stewardship records and legal share-class mappings.
  • Time-series holdings showing that dominance cells persist rather than reflect a single snapshot.
  • Risk-contribution estimates demonstrating that value share materially overstates or understates attention needs.
