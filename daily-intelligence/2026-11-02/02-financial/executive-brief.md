# Executive Brief | Financial Intelligence

## The ECB Hold and the Two-Sided Margin Squeeze

**02 November 2026 | Independent institutional research**

**The decision question.** Can a bank preserve net interest income if its deposit beta rises while the policy-rate path remains uncertain?

**Executive assessment.** The ECB’s 29 October decision held policy rates constant while explicitly recognising a difficult combination of energy-driven inflation risk and softer growth. For financial institutions the immediate issue is not the policy level alone but asymmetric repricing of funding and assets. The calculator illustrates the sign and magnitude of first-order annual net-interest-income sensitivity under different deposit and asset betas. Even with a positive rate shock, a sufficiently large deposit beta can erode NII. The model deliberately excludes hedging and behavioural deposit dynamics; those are precisely the controls that an ALCO should reconcile before translating the signal into pricing or duration decisions.

**Worked example.** Illustrative worked example: €1.5bn of assets, €1.3bn of liabilities, a +100bp parallel shock, 50% asset beta and 65% deposit beta yield −€0.95m annual ΔNII. The deposit beta break-even is approximately 57.7%.

**Recommended action.** Monitor deposit and asset repricing betas alongside stress-driven liquidity migration. Avoid extrapolating the ECB’s unchanged decision into a forecast of future rates.

**Evidence constraints.** All balance-sheet amounts, betas and shocks are hypothetical. Parallel instantaneous repricing assumes a one-year static balance sheet, no hedges, floors, behavioural deposits, credit migration or FX. September HICP is an earlier release and is not an October flash estimate.
