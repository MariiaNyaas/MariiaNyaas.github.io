# Executive Brief | Credit Risk Intelligence

## Norway’s Mortgage Risk Is Moving Towards the Boundary

**02 November 2026 | Independent institutional research**

**The decision question.** How much borrower equity remains after a housing correction when lending shifts closer to the 90% LTV limit?

**Executive assessment.** The 2026 Finanstilsynet survey shows a higher average debt burden and a shift of new lending towards leverage limits. The change is not, by itself, evidence of imminent credit deterioration: a larger share near a regulatory boundary can coexist with improving affordability in other parts of the book. The relevant risk-management question is the convexity of collateral protection under house-price stress. This interactive case translates origination LTV into stressed collateral buffers without asserting that the aggregate borrower has the first-time-buyer profile. A lender should segment newly originated exposures by LTV, DTI, region and debt-servicing headroom before adjusting risk appetite.

**Worked example.** Illustrative worked example: a NOK 5.0m home financed at 79% LTV carries NOK 3.95m of debt. A 20% price decline leaves NOK 50,000 collateral equity and produces a 98.75% stressed LTV.

**Recommended action.** Use origination LTV bands and stressed collateral buffers jointly, rather than relying on the average debt-to-income ratio alone. Validate results with loan-level affordability, guarantees and geographic exposures.

**Evidence constraints.** Survey of approximately 12,000 new loans from 25 banks, not a full mortgage stock. Observed averages describe different cohorts. The model applies hypothetical LTV and price shocks to a single property; it is not an estimate of bank losses, PD or LGD.
