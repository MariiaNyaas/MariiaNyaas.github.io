# Methodology — Europe’s Positive GDP Print Conceals a Slower Impulse

**Edition:** 2026-11-02  \n**Status:** Independent analysis, primary-source snapshot.

## Research question

How sensitive is a Nordic exporter’s revenue to the slowdown in European quarterly growth?

## Official observations and trigger

Eurostat’s 30 October flash estimate showed euro-area GDP growth easing from 0.6% q/q in Q2 to 0.4% in Q3, and EU growth easing from 0.7% to 0.5%.

- EA Q2: **0.6 % q/q**
- EA Q3: **0.4 % q/q**
- EU Q2: **0.7 % q/q**
- EU Q3: **0.5 % q/q**

## Reproducible model

Observed growth momentum = Q3 q/q growth − Q2 q/q growth (−0.2 pp for both EU and EA). Illustrative revenue delta (€m) = annual exposed revenue × elasticity × (observed momentum + additional scenario shock) / 100. No causal estimate or time-series regression is implied.

**Worked example:** Illustrative worked example: the observed −0.2 percentage-point change in euro-area quarterly GDP growth, applied with an assumed 1.5× revenue elasticity to €1bn of annual exposed sales, yields a −€3m sensitivity. It is not a revenue forecast.

All user controls are hypothetical analyst-defined inputs. Recalculate in browser by changing the controls; the model is contained in the self-contained `index.html`. The data snapshot is in `data.json`.

## Limitations and validation boundaries

GDP is a preliminary flash estimate subject to revision; growth figures are quarter-on-quarter, not annual rates. Elasticity and annual revenue are hypothetical; the scenario applies a quarterly GDP-growth difference mechanically to an annual sales base solely for sensitivity illustration.

## Management decision

Use leading order-book indicators and customer concentration to test whether European growth deceleration affects the next two quarters of sales. Do not treat aggregate GDP as a direct export-demand forecast.
