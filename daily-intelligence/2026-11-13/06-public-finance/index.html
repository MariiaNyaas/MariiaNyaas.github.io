The Revenue–Spending Asymmetry

What historical revenue contractions do—and do not—say about fiscal adjustment

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. The consolidated general-government sample has 30 annual observations, 1995–2024, and 29 adjacent-year growth observations.
  2. Nominal revenue contracts in seven years: 1998, 2002, 2009, 2013, 2015, 2020 and 2023.
  3. Nominal expenditure rises in all seven revenue-contraction years. This is a descriptive pattern, not an estimate of discretionary stimulus.
  4. Revenue minus expenditure reconciles with reported net lending within one NOK million in every sampled year.

The mechanism and the test

Government spending is less mechanically tied to current receipts than a household budget. Automatic stabilisers, nominal commitments, petroleum-related revenue and discretionary policy can all separate the paths. That makes a headline surplus an incomplete measure of adjustment capacity. The study concerns revenue/spending asymmetry, not yesterday’s hypothetical AI-energy fiscal dividend.

Methodology & limitations

SSB 10721, consolidated General government only, NOK million, 1995–2024. Do not add central and local totals. Adjacent growth = value_t/value_(t−1)−1. Identify contraction strictly at negative nominal revenue growth and inspect concurrent nominal expenditure growth. Reconcile A−E−F within one source unit. The Spearman rank statistic is exploratory and not a causal response estimate. Annual sample is short and regime dependent; no fiscal multiplier, sovereign default model or structural forecast is estimated. Laboratory anchors to a selected observed year, compounds an assumed annual revenue decline and spending increase, and reports nominal net lending in NOK billion.

Nominal general-government flows through 2024; petroleum exposure, inflation and policy confound associations. Not the structural non-oil budget balance or fiscal-rule headroom; no sovereign PD inference. Latest observation is 2024, not 2026. No claim about today’s structural non-oil budget, fiscal-rule compliance, available cash or fiscal space. No GDP deflator, output gap or commodity-price instrument is supplied; real or causal adjustment estimates are NOT VERIFIED.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • Structural non-oil and cyclically adjusted series overturning the nominal pattern.
  • A decomposition separating automatic stabilisers, petroleum receipts and policy decisions.
  • New comparable annual data showing expenditure contracts consistently with revenue.
