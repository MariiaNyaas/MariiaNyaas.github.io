The In-House AI Capability Gap

Eurostat isoc_eb_ai export, extracted 13 November 2026, last updated 15 June 2026. Select the exact indicator “Enterprises' AI technologies were developed by own employees”, not the any-AI adoption indicator. Preserve the source unit label “Percentage of enterprises”, size category, NACE scope, year and flags. Compare 10–49 with 250+ employee categories within identical geography and year. The 10+ overall category is reported directly, never reconstructed by averaging size classes. Available survey years are 2021/2023/2024/2025; missing cells remain missing, including Denmark 2025. Gap = large-category share minus small-category share, in percentage points. No division by adoption or inference about the fraction of AI users, external procurement or vendor dependence is made. Repeated cross-sections cannot identify a causal scale effect. The laboratory adds user-assumed annual pp gains from each country’s latest paired observation, with a 100% ceiling; it is not a fitted forecast.

Limits of identification

Source percentages and survey populations are retained; no conditional AI-user denominator is inferred. Denmark 2025 is missing. Sweden 2023 carries a source break flag. No matched firm panel, sampling standard errors, employment weights, AI expenditure or skill-cost data are supplied. Consequently causal estimates, statistical confidence intervals and forecast validation are NOT VERIFIED and are not claimed. Enterprises below 10 employees and the financial sector are outside the stated survey perimeter.

Audit scope

Separate calculation paths re-read source records. This is automated review by the same AI session, not external human peer review. Publication checks are itemised in the audit.
