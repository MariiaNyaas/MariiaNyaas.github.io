The In-House AI Capability Gap

Who develops AI inside the enterprise—and why adoption alone cannot answer that question?

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. Norway’s 2025 reported own-employee AI-development share is 3.91% for enterprises with 10–49 employees and 24.59% for enterprises with 250+ employees: a 20.68 percentage-point gap.
  2. The comparable 2025 gap is 19.38 pp in Sweden (5.50% versus 24.88%) and 26.04 pp in Finland (4.96% versus 31.00%).
  3. Denmark has no usable 2025 observation for this indicator in the supplied export. Its latest paired values are from 2024: 4.89% and 22.52%, a 17.63 pp gap.
  4. Norway’s all-enterprise-size 10+ reported share rises from 3.00% in 2023 to 5.25% in 2025. This measures the source’s own-development indicator, not a share of AI users or outsourced spending.

The mechanism and the test

Adopting an AI product and developing AI with employees are different organisational capabilities. Internal development may require complementary skills, governance and scale. The reported size gradient is consistent with that mechanism, but sector composition and selection can also produce it. This new question examines own-employee development; yesterday’s study examined adoption and productivity. An enterprise can both develop internally and buy external technology, so absence of internal development is not evidence of outsourcing.

Methodology & limitations

Eurostat isoc_eb_ai export, extracted 13 November 2026, last updated 15 June 2026. Select the exact indicator “Enterprises' AI technologies were developed by own employees”, not the any-AI adoption indicator. Preserve the source unit label “Percentage of enterprises”, size category, NACE scope, year and flags. Compare 10–49 with 250+ employee categories within identical geography and year. The 10+ overall category is reported directly, never reconstructed by averaging size classes. Available survey years are 2021/2023/2024/2025; missing cells remain missing, including Denmark 2025. Gap = large-category share minus small-category share, in percentage points. No division by adoption or inference about the fraction of AI users, external procurement or vendor dependence is made. Repeated cross-sections cannot identify a causal scale effect. The laboratory adds user-assumed annual pp gains from each country’s latest paired observation, with a 100% ceiling; it is not a fitted forecast.

Source percentages and survey populations are retained; no conditional AI-user denominator is inferred. Denmark 2025 is missing. Sweden 2023 carries a source break flag. No matched firm panel, sampling standard errors, employment weights, AI expenditure or skill-cost data are supplied. Consequently causal estimates, statistical confidence intervals and forecast validation are NOT VERIFIED and are not claimed. Enterprises below 10 employees and the financial sector are outside the stated survey perimeter.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • A stable-definition enterprise panel overturning the within-country size gradient.
  • Survey denominator documentation or revisions materially changing interpretation.
  • Evidence that reported internal development reflects classification rather than organisational capability.
