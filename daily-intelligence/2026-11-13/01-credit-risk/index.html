The Bankruptcy Acceleration Test

Can a three-month distress signal beat the seasonal calendar?

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. The Oslo series has 226 monthly bankruptcy-count observations; the latest observation is 2026-10.
  2. Across 36 overlapping three-month forecast origins, candidate MAE is 25.60 cases versus 28.50 for the same three months a year earlier.
  3. The 6-origin block-bootstrap 95% interval for the mean MAE improvement is −5.81 to +11.87 cases. A positive point estimate does not establish predictive superiority.
  4. The latest trailing-12-month total is 691 cases, a -19.5% change from the preceding 12 months.

The mechanism and the test

A quarterly distress signal aggregates noisy monthly cases, but overlapping targets create dependence. Better aggregation is useful only if a forecasting rule survives a genuine forward test. The 11 November financial stability review identifies pressure among property developers; this aggregate Oslo exercise cannot identify that sectoral mechanism.

Methodology & limitations

SSB 08551, first exported all-industry row, exact total-bankruptcy columns for Oslo code 03. Freeze six regressors: constant, preceding count, preceding 12-month mean, preceding three-month sum and two calendar harmonics. Predict the next three-month sum by OLS. At each origin purge the two most recent training targets because their three-month windows are not yet complete. Use the last 36 origins; compare with the same three months one year earlier. Forecasts are floored at zero. Moving circular bootstrap of paired absolute-error differences: 2,000 draws, seed 20261113, blocks 3/6/12. Latest-vintage holdout, not real-time archival backtesting. Specification is exploratory; no hypothesis-testing claim.

Counts are not PD; Oslo only; no firm or credit denominator. Candidate fitted after seeing dataset; exploratory, not preregistered. Counts are cases, not rates or credit losses. Bootstrap intervals do not correct specification selection. The laboratory is a transparent scaling of the latest observed 12-month average, not the estimated forecasting model.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • A preregistered rolling evaluation with new observations and a persistent loss advantage.
  • Borrower-level exposure denominators and sector-consistent records supporting actual credit-risk estimation.
  • An advantage that survives structural-break and reporting-change treatment.
