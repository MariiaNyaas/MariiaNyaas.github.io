The Electricity–Credit Nexus

When an energy constraint becomes a financing constraint

Power prices matter for debt capacity through cash flow, hedging and pass-through. Public aggregate observations do not identify a causal electricity–default elasticity.

Economic mechanism

How the transmission works

The cash-flow channel

An electricity-price shock first affects the unhedged energy bill. The borrower absorbs only the share that cannot be passed to customers. Lower operating cash flow then competes with interest and principal repayments. Debt capacity depends on that residual exposure, rather than the headline spot price.

The physical channel

A connection delay or curtailment can reduce output even under a fixed-price contract. Contract tenor, counterparty strength, curtailment rights and local network availability therefore belong in underwriting. ENTSO-E installed capacity and cross-border flows provide system context, not a site-specific guarantee of firm power.

The identification problem

Bankruptcy counts combine firm formation, insolvency rules, sector composition, refinancing conditions and reporting. The regional export uses modern county codes with historical zeros. We select the continuous Oslo geography and all industries, excluding personal-bankruptcy columns and sector subcategories. We do not interpret these observations as a nationwide firm default panel.

Methodology

Reproducible, within limits

Source: SSB 08551, all industries, total bankruptcies, Oslo code 03. Sort 226 months; do not sum county series with historical zeros. Model log(1+B_t)=a+b log(1+B_(t−1))+c sin(2πm/12)+d cos(2πm/12). Fit by OLS at each expanding origin, forecasting one month ahead over the last 36 months (2023-11–2026-10). Exponentiation gives a conditional-median count forecast, without a lognormal mean correction. Compare against B_(t−12); report MAE/RMSE. Full-sample coefficient intervals use Bartlett Newey–West covariance with 12 lags. No electricity regressor enters this baseline. The sector price scenarios are arithmetic, not estimated PD forecasts.

No firm-level energy exposure, outstanding-credit denominator, borrower failure panel or matched hedge information. Covid-era policy and insolvency changes are potential breaks. Baseline forecast evaluation uses a single holdout; specification selection was not preregistered. No causal or nationwide predictive validation is claimed.

What Would Change Our Mind

The evidence that could overturn the thesis

  • A matched firm-level panel linking electricity invoices and contracts to arrears/defaults, with pre-shock exposure and a credible comparison group.
  • A geography-consistent multi-year price panel and rolling tests showing improvement over rates, sector and macro controls.
  • Evidence that physical curtailment, rather than energy costs, dominates borrower cash losses.
