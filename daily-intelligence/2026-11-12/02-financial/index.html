The Economics of Stored Electricity

The value of flexibility, after the foresight premium

Battery economics depend on when energy can be sold and which revenues are executable. Perfect-foresight arbitrage materially overstates the performance of a frozen dispatch rule.

Chronological holdout · gross-spread experimentIndependent research · no NBIM affiliation

  1. At 90% efficiency, NO1’s frozen schedule earns 151.79 NOK per 1 MWh daily charge in the 184-block holdout, before all operating and capital costs.
  2. The same holdout yields a 475.86 NOK/day perfect-foresight upper bound. The frozen rule captures 31.9% of that gross opportunity.
  3. A 2,000-draw circular seven-day block bootstrap puts the NO1 mean between 69.68 and 238.41 NOK/day at the 95% level. This is sampling uncertainty, not a future-return interval.
  4. At 80% efficiency the NO1 fixed-rule mean falls to 66.48 NOK/day. Excluding the final source block changes the 90% result to 153.01.

Economic mechanism

How the transmission works

Storage is a timing asset

The asset buys flexibility between a charging opportunity and a later delivery opportunity. High average prices alone do not guarantee a useful spread. Efficiency losses can make a seemingly attractive pair unprofitable; grid tariffs and throughput costs raise the minimum required spread.

The foresight premium

An optimiser that observes the complete realised day can choose the best charge/discharge pair and avoid bad days. That is an information advantage. Our frozen schedule chooses an ordered pair using only the first 181 blocks, then dispatches unconditionally in the remaining 184. It therefore accepts losses on adverse days instead of silently selecting profitable hindsight trades.

The revenue stack has limits

The workbook includes reserve-market price and volume sheets. They are inventoried, but their revenues are not added to spot arbitrage: qualification, capacity reservation, activation energy, state-of-charge constraints and mutually exclusive commitments would need a joint operational model. NBIM’s November Aypa investment motivates the question; Norwegian prices do not value a North American platform.

Methodology

Reproducible, within limits

NTNU-hosted Power Market NO 2025 workbook, Strømpris sheet: 8,760 indexed records × five zones. Convert øre/kWh to NOK/MWh by ×10. Partition each zone into 365 sequential 24-index blocks; the workbook lacks timestamp/time-zone columns. Train on blocks 0–180. Evaluate all 276 ordered pairs i<j and choose the pair maximising average 0.9×p_j−p_i in training. Freeze that pair for blocks 181–364. A cycle charges 1 MWh in one hour and delivers 0.9 MWh in a later hour; initial and final state are zero. The hindsight benchmark takes max(0,max_(i<j)(0.9p_j−p_i)); it is an upper bound for this restricted one-cycle model, not all conceivable battery operations. Bootstrap 184 holdout daily outcomes in circular seven-block clusters, 2,000 draws with fixed seed 20261112. Efficiency sensitivity holds the original schedule fixed. NPV repeats the held-out mean as a scenario, applies cost/availability/fade assumptions and discounts annual cash flows.

One year, one chronological split, no seasonal replication. The hosting institution is NTNU; underlying prices are compiled from Nord Pool/Statnett/Norges Bank. The final all-zone zero hour is retained and flagged. The workbook MD5 is checked against the repository record. Exact timestamps and daylight-saving treatment are not authenticated. Reserve sheets and FX are not treated as executable revenue. A 1 MWh charge-equivalent unit is not a fully specified battery procurement quote.

What Would Change Our Mind

The evidence that could overturn the thesis

  • A cost-inclusive, timestamp-verified out-of-sample policy with durable positive cash flow over multiple years.
  • Market saturation or reduced volatility compressing executable spreads.
  • A credible contracted revenue floor or reserve co-optimisation changing the risk-adjusted result.
