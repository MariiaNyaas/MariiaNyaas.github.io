# Methodology: The Wage-Commitment Audit: Why One-Off Pay Distorts 2027 Cost Estimates

Publication: 2026-11-09
Primary release: ECB wage tracker, 4 Nov 2026 — https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr261104~caf181d7ac.en.html

## Observed facts
- H2 2026 headline: 2.7%
- H1 2027 headline: 2.8%
- Q3 2027 headline: 2.4%
- H1 2027 coverage: 31.8%

## Hypothesis
Wage agreements can create a timing and recognition problem: headline negotiated growth and permanent recurring pay need not match.

## Deterministic model
recurringAnnualCost=payrollBase×recurringUplift/100; oneOffCost=payrollBase×oneOffComponent/100

## Inputs
- Annual payroll base: 250€m (range 10–1000)
- Recurring wage uplift: 2.8% (range 0–8)
- One-off component: 0.5% (range 0–5)

## Interpretation and limitations
Scenario uses user-entered payroll and rates; ECB tracker is an aggregate negotiation indicator, not a firm-specific accounting accrual.

## Competing explanation
Coverage of the ECB tracker varies materially by horizon; one-off timing, headcount and social costs affect actual accruals.

## Falsification
Payroll records, contracts and accrual schedules reconcile recurring and one-off payments without unexplained variances.

## Research status
Exploratory scenario, not causal identification, calibrated forecast, portfolio recommendation or empirical backtest. Observed indicators must not be treated as the model's calibrated parameters.
