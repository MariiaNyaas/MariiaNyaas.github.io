# Research Intelligence Brief — The Nordic Margin Transmission Test

**Central thesis (hypothesis, not established causality):** a broad producer-price headline can obscure a concentrated energy shock; the economic consequence depends on a company's procurement mix, price-recovery capacity and cost structure.

## Verified numerical findings
1. Euro-area total industrial PPI increased **2.5% m/m** in September 2026 (Eurostat, 5 November 2026).
2. Euro-area energy PPI increased **7.8% m/m**, while total industry excluding energy rose **0.1% m/m**: a **7.7 percentage-point** component gap, calculated as 7.8−0.1.
3. September total-industry national PPI rose **3.6% Denmark**, **3.5% Sweden**, **2.6% Finland** and **2.5% Norway** (monthly rates, not comparable energy intensities).
4. Euro-area energy PPI rose **30.8% y/y** versus **3.5% y/y** excluding energy in September: **27.3 percentage points** difference.
5. Euro-area total PPI rose **11.1% y/y** in September, distinct from its 2.5% monthly increase.

Source: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-euro-indicators/w/4-05112026-ap.

## Investor / decision matrix
- Energy accelerates while ex-energy stays flat → possible concentrated input-cost shock; confirm company invoices, energy procurement and hedges.
- Both energy and ex-energy accelerate → possible diffusion; confirm intermediate-goods and negotiated sales prices.
- National PPI diverges without firm cost divergence → industry composition / FX explanation; obtain company-level costs before revising valuation.

## What would change our mind?
Sustained ex-energy acceleration, lack of correspondence between PPI and actual invoices, or evidence that contracted energy prices shield costs. The model is a conditional scenario, not a causal estimate.

## Decision implication
Stress-test margin under different energy exposure, cost ratios and recovery assumptions; prioritize contractual and invoice evidence over the headline aggregate.
