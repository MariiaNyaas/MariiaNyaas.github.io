Corporate Credit Cycles and Bankruptcy Risk

Does slower corporate credit expansion coincide with more business failures?

Research status: exploratory empirical study. Historical descriptive estimates and scenario sensitivities are not causal forecasts. Data coverage, comparability and model limitations are disclosed below.

Research intelligence brief

Across 1998–2025, an exploratory contemporaneous regression relates a 1 percentage-point increase in annual corporate credit growth to approximately 57 fewer bankruptcies (HAC 95% CI −64.70 to −49.33; R²≈0.555). This is not a causal effect: business cycles, sector mix, reporting changes and reverse causality remain unresolved.

Model validation and forward-looking limitations

Corrected source definition: SSB 09560, December bank loans to non-financial corporations; year-on-year percentage changes; SSB 09695, sum of monthly bankruptcies. Sample 1998–2025 (n=28).

Same-year OLS, HAC(2): coefficient −57.02 bankruptcies per 1 percentage-point change in credit growth, 95% confidence interval [−64.70, −49.33], R²=0.555. This is contemporaneous, not a leading signal.

One-year-lag model: coefficient −32.96, p≈0.019, R²=0.194 (n=27). Two-year lag coefficient −2.74, p≈0.803 (n=26). Lag stability is weak.

Expanding-window test: for one-year-lag prediction, RMSE ≈515 bankruptcies versus ≈565 for a historical-mean benchmark. The sample is small and subject to macroeconomic confounding, reporting changes and reverse causality. No causal or deployment-grade early-warning claim is justified.

Decision use: treat credit growth as a contextual risk signal alongside sector-level default rates, arrears, insolvency exposures and economic controls; do not assign capital or limits using this coefficient alone.

Methodology, source and limitations

Source: Statistics Norway, tables 09560 and 09695. Annual December credit stocks across reported lender categories, percentage change; annual sum of bankruptcy counts. Association is contemporaneous, not predictive.

Calculations use historical observations through 2025, except the initial 2026 public R&D budget which is explicitly a budget allocation rather than a realised outcome. Recalculated Pearson correlations use pairwise observations in the chosen time window. Rebased values divide each selected series by its first value and multiply by 100; zero baseline is excluded. Scenario results are deterministic and do not incorporate confidence intervals, dynamic feedback, changing composition, omitted factors or out-of-sample validation.
