The Policy-Rate Signal Test

Can the Norwegian policy rate improve a monthly EUR/NOK random-walk baseline?

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. The retained monthly history runs through 2026-10: EUR/NOK 10.5914 and an average policy rate of 4.50%.
  2. Across 71 expanding monthly origins, the signal model has MAE 1.406% versus 1.471% for a zero-return random walk.
  3. Directional accuracy is 59.2%.
  4. The three-month-block 95% interval for mean MAE improvement is -0.032 to 0.168 percentage points; it crosses zero.

The mechanism and the test

A higher domestic rate can support a currency through carry, yet the same rate can signal inflation or macro stress. EUR/NOK also absorbs foreign rates, energy prices and global risk appetite. A statistically richer story earns credibility only if it improves the next-month random-walk baseline.

Methodology & limitations

Norges Bank monthly EUR/NOK middle rates and daily key policy-rate observations, January 2000–October 2026. Aggregate the policy rate to monthly mean; compute monthly log EUR/NOK changes. At every one of the final 71 origins, re-estimate next-month change on the current change, current rate change and current rate level using only earlier rows. Compare with zero return. Circular three-month blocks, 5,000 draws, seed 20261122.

Monthly Norges Bank middle rates are indicative, not executable returns. The model omits foreign rates, risk appetite, energy prices and intervention expectations. The 72-month evaluation is latest-vintage and repeatedly re-estimated; it is not a trading strategy and includes no transaction costs.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • Foreign policy rates, energy prices and risk factors in a pre-specified model.
  • A stable net advantage after spreads, slippage and new untouched months.
  • Evidence that the effect survives alternative horizons and rate aggregations.
