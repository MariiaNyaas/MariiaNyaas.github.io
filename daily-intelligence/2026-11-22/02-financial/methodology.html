From currency evidence to a defensible decision.

How do exposure, hedge terms, business uncertainty and liquidity constraints change the feasible decision when a reliable directional FX advantage has not been established?

Three distinct layers. Official observations describe the market. Deterministic scenarios calculate consequences of explicit assumptions. Historical forecasting evidence tests whether a statistical model adds information. A scenario receives no probability merely because it appears on a chart.

Treasury: reconcile the money

For a cash flow, A is the original foreign-currency amount; Q the scenario amount actually occurring; H the fixed contracted hedge amount; B the budget rate; F the contract forward rate; and S the scenario settlement rate. Rates are NOK per one foreign-currency unit. The sign d = +1 for a payable and d = −1 for a receivable.

Net NOK cash cost = d × [Q × S + H × (F − S)]
Budget = d × A × B
Volume effect = d × (Q − A) × B
Unhedged FX effect = d × Q × (S − B)
Hedge effect = d × H × (F − S)

Budget plus these effects must reconcile to net cash cost. A positive deviation is adverse for this signed cost measure. A forecast-volume shock does not resize an already contracted hedge. Reduced or cancelled forecasts can produce overhedging, which is reported explicitly. Committed flows are not changed by a forecast-only volume shock.

The settlement timeline preserves due dates. Opposite flows are not automatically operationally interchangeable because their currencies match. Entity and timing information remain visible; a group-level sum does not authorise intercompany transfers or establish legal netting.

Policy comparison and reverse stress

The current hedge policy is the decision comparator. Alternative fixed hedge shares use the same exposures, scenario and forward-rate assumptions. This counterfactual comparison does not imply that existing contracts can be cancelled or resized without cost.

For a certain payable with hedge share h:
Cash cost = A × [h × F + (1 − h) × S]
With allowed budget overrun L, the boundary is:
A × [h × F + (1 − h) × S − B] = L.

The solver includes upper bounds, empty feasible sets and fully feasible intervals where appropriate. At 100% hedging the settlement FX sensitivity of a certain fixed payment is zero; cancellation, counterparty exposure and collateral constraints can remain. A common-FX shock boundary is a deterministic joint stress, not a probability forecast.

Random walk is a forecasting benchmark. A user's stated policy is the decision benchmark. Forward points, execution spreads, fees and financing terms for the intended date are needed before an indicative comparison becomes an executable transaction analysis.

Portfolio: separate assets and currencies

For initial foreign-currency position V, initial reference rate S₀, local asset return r and FX change f:

Unhedged NOK return = (1 + r) × (1 + f) − 1 = r + f + r × f
Local-asset effect = V × S₀ × r
Currency effect = V × S₀ × f
Interaction = V × S₀ × r × f
Fixed-unit FX hedge payoff = H × (F − S)

The interaction term is retained. Assumed returns of −20% on a local asset and +15% on its currency produce −8% in NOK before hedge effects. This does not prove future diversification.

The policy benchmark uses the same positions with a stated benchmark hedge share. It is not the actual GPFG benchmark. Denomination is contractual currency exposure; for equities it does not capture all issuer revenues, costs, debt and competitive exposure. Example holdings are not a full economic look-through model.

NBIM measures return in NOK and its benchmark currency basket. Krone translation and international purchasing power have different interpretations. NBIM return methodology.

Liquidity and collateral

Collateral sensitivity is separate from terminal cash cost. Explicit stressed forward marks can be supplied. In their absence the engine labels a simplified, undiscounted settlement-payoff proxy. This is not a dealer mark-to-market and does not reconstruct yield curves, cross-currency basis or a complete CSA valuation model.

Derivative marks offset only within explicitly identified entity/netting-set groups. Thresholds, posted collateral and eligible noncash collateral are assumptions. A haircut reduces eligible collateral value. Cash reserves and facilities need to be available in the same stress. Collateral calls are financing requirements, not automatically additional terminal losses.

The settlement timeline and immediate collateral need are separate views. They do not assert a complete intraday liquidity forecast, future margin path, legal enforceability or funding availability. Apply actual positions, counterparty terms and stressed forward marks to a specific institution.

Primary evidence: Norges Bank survey, 2025 Q1 and FSB liquidity preparedness report.

Scenario coherence and historical replays

Observed replays calculate joint FX changes between exact recorded dates, then apply them hypothetically to the current chosen book and contract assumptions. They do not reconstruct historical trades. An FX-only replay supplies no invented equity or bond return. Daily inputs and exact endpoints are retained for verification.

Assumed stress presets are labelled separately. Inputs are interpretable market and business variables; derived macro features such as log VIX are not independent business-scenario controls. Local-asset returns, FX changes and volume assumptions remain visible.

Retained forecasting evidence

The experiment evaluates cumulative log EUR/NOK returns from month-end reference fixings, using expanding training windows, matched evaluation origins, only matured target labels, train-only scaling and fixed ridge and ensemble parameters. Brent, VIX and dollar predictors are lagged one month; inflation and industrial production two months. Latest-vintage revisions remain a limitation.

The separate ECB module has 292 historical events through October 2025, one prespecified OIS tenor and fixing windows strictly after each event. All three Bonferroni-adjusted intervals include zero. OIS repricing includes policy, guidance and information effects; it is not an isolated policy-rate hike.
