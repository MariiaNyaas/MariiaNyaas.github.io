The Interest-Burden Threshold Test

When household interest intensity rises, does reported payment strain become more predictable?

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. In the latest matched year (2024), 20.0% of households paid at least 15% of total income in interest; 5.4% of persons reported housing-payment problems.
  2. Across 8 expanding one-year origins, the burden-plus-persistence rule records MAE 0.573 pp versus 0.475 pp for persistence.
  3. The candidate is worse by 0.098 pp on the point estimate.
  4. The 95% two-year-block interval for baseline-minus-model improvement is -0.318 to 0.050 pp; predictive superiority is not established.

The mechanism and the test

High interest payments compress household cash flow, but distributional burden and realised payment problems are not the same object. Income recovery, liquid buffers and refinancing can break the apparent pass-through. The test therefore asks whether the burden share adds forward information beyond simply carrying the last strain reading forward.

Methodology & limitations

SSB 08727: household share by interest paid as a percentage of total income, 2006–2024. Sum the disjoint 15–20, 20–30, 30–40 and 40+ bins. SSB 12128: percentage reporting problems paying housing costs, all income groups, 2011–2025. Match common years. At each origin fit next-year strain on the current strain and current ≥15% burden share using only earlier rows; compare with persistence. Retain the last eight eligible origins. Circular two-year blocks, 5,000 draws, seed 20261122.

Household percentages are survey/distribution measures, not exposure-weighted arrears, PD or loss. The interest-burden table ends in 2024 and the housing-payment series in 2025; the forward test is latest-vintage, not an archived real-time backtest. Household categories are not borrower records.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • Exposure-weighted arrears and balances tied to the same households.
  • An untouched sequence of survey vintages with a stable loss advantage.
  • A result robust to alternative burden thresholds, income groups and real-time revisions.
