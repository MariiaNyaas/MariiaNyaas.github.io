The Sovereign Interest-Pass-Through Test

Can the policy rate improve Norway’s next-year net-interest baseline?

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. In 2024, consolidated government interest income was NOK 254.54bn and interest expense NOK 79.75bn.
  2. The resulting net interest position was NOK 174.79bn with an annual-average policy rate of 4.50%.
  3. Across the final ten origins, the rate-signal model has MAE NOK 17.03bn versus NOK 15.94bn for persistence.
  4. The two-year-block 95% interval for baseline-minus-model improvement is NOK -5.00bn to 2.99bn; it crosses zero.

The mechanism and the test

A higher policy rate does not reprice public assets and liabilities symmetrically. Maturity, currency and asset composition determine pass-through. The test asks whether a coarse rate signal adds next-year forecasting information beyond the observed net interest position.

Methodology & limitations

SSB 10721, consolidated general government, nominal NOK million, interest income A51 and interest expense B41, 1995–2024. Norges Bank daily key policy rate aggregated to annual mean from 2000. Net position = income − expense. At each origin fit next-year net interest on current net interest, current rate level and rate change using only earlier rows; compare with persistence. Retain final ten origins. Circular two-year blocks, 5,000 draws.

Nominal consolidated general-government interest flows are not the structural non-oil balance, fiscal-rule headroom, debt duration or sovereign solvency. Annual average policy rates are a coarse signal; asset composition, currency, maturity, GPFG income and accounting classifications are omitted. Latest-vintage, not real-time-vintage validation.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • Instrument-level maturity, currency and duration data.
  • Real-time fiscal vintages and an untouched new year.
  • A stable advantage after separating GPFG and mainland government exposures.
