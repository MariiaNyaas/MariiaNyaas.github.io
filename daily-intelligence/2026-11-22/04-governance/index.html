The Voice-to-Capital Mismatch

Where reported voting rights and economic ownership cease to be interchangeable

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. The 30 June 2026 NBIM equity snapshot contains 7,077 holdings with both voting and ownership percentages.
  2. The capital-weighted absolute voting–ownership gap is 0.093 percentage points; the raw correlation is 0.981.
  3. At a 0.10 pp gap threshold, 410 holdings qualify and represent 12.89% of analysed market value.
  4. Value with a voting discount beyond 0.10 pp represents 11.02% of value, versus 1.87% with a voting premium.

The mechanism and the test

Stewardship teams often use capital exposure as a proxy for voice. Dual-class structures, share lending and market rules can break that equivalence. The map identifies where a value-based queue may misstate formal voting capacity; it does not infer influence or governance quality.

Methodology & limitations

NBIM listed-equity holdings report dated 30 June 2026, UTF-16 semicolon-separated CSV. Retain rows with numeric market value, voting percentage and ownership percentage. Gap = voting − ownership; report absolute gap, value-weighted absolute gap and threshold sensitivity at 0.10/0.25/0.50/1.00/2.00 pp. Denominator is market value of retained rows. Snapshot analysis; forecast backtesting is not applicable.

One 30 June 2026 holdings snapshot. Reported voting and ownership percentages are not legal opinions, expected influence, engagement intensity or realised governance outcomes. Share classes, lending, local rules and meeting-specific eligibility may matter; no time-series persistence test is possible from this extract.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • Time-series holdings proving that gaps persist.
  • Engagement outcomes demonstrating that formal vote gaps alter stewardship effectiveness.
