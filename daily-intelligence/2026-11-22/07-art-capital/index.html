The Museum Subsidy Resilience Test

Public funding, own revenue and the limits of predicting cultural demand

EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE · EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS · USER SCENARIOS

Research Intelligence Brief

  1. In 2024, Norwegian museums recorded 11.53 million individual-plus-group visits.
  2. Public contributions were NOK 5.57bn, equal to 71.1% of total nominal revenue.
  3. Own revenue was NOK 2.26bn, or approximately NOK 196 per visit.
  4. Across the final ten origins, the subsidy-share model has MAE 1.442m visits versus 1.319m for persistence; the interval crosses zero.

The mechanism and the test

Public subsidy can stabilise cultural institutions without mechanically generating visitors. Funding may rise precisely when earned income or attendance is weak, creating reverse causality. The study separates financial resilience from demand prediction and treats the subsidy share as a signal, not an intervention effect.

Methodology & limitations

SSB 09542 national museum revenue, expenditure and public contributions, and SSB 09539 visits, 2001–2024. Total visits = individual visits + group visits; paying visits overlap and are not added. Subsidy share = public contributions / total revenue; own revenue = total revenue − public contributions. At each origin fit next-year total visits on current visits and subsidy share using only earlier rows; compare with persistence. Retain final ten origins; report pandemic-excluded MAE separately. Circular two-year blocks, 5,000 draws.

National museum aggregates combine institutions and changing populations. Individual and group visits are summed as disjoint categories; paying visits overlap and are not added. Nominal revenue is not deflated. Public contributions may respond to need, creating endogeneity; the exercise is predictive, not causal. The 2025 questionnaire revision prevents extending the same visit definition without a documented bridge.

What Would Change Our Mind

  • Institution-level panel data with consistent museum population.
  • Deflated revenue, ticket prices and exhibition-level demand.
  • An exogenous funding design separating subsidy causality from need-based allocation.
