The Resale Right Paradox

Who bears the cost of artists' resale royalties? An interactive investigation of royalty schedules, price thresholds, transaction economics, collector returns and the UK's 2024 regulatory currency change.

UK primary legislation10+ independent variablesEight analytical laboratoriesDeterministic, reproducible modelsNo fabricated auction transactions
Research intelligence brief

Four findings worth investigating

Regulatory discontinuity
1 April 2024

UK thresholds and bands switched from EUR to GBP; the numerical thresholds remained unchanged. A currency-denomination change is not evidence of a causal price effect.

Cumulative royalty rate
4% → 0.25%

Marginal rates decline by price band; the royalty is capped at £12,500 per eligible sale.

Identification challenge
Not established

Observed prices alone cannot reveal who economically bears the royalty. Supply and demand elasticities, selection and contract terms matter.

UK legal parameters: UK Intellectual Property Office and DACS. The 2024 regulatory change and impact assumptions are documented in the UK explanatory memorandum.

08 / Research agenda & decision intelligence

What would establish an actual price effect?

Identification strategy

Construct transaction-level records for eligible and ineligible artists around the April 2024 reform. Use artist fixed effects, auction-house controls, work characteristics, exchange-rate exposure, sale selection and time effects. Test pre-trends, manipulation around the threshold, placebo cutoffs and heterogeneous effects.

A difference-in-differences or regression-discontinuity design would require defensible comparison groups and careful treatment of currency and eligibility changes.

Investment committee questions

Is the work eligible? Who contractually bears ARR? How does royalty affect the investor's net exit return? Does an observed price difference survive controls for artist reputation, medium, size, provenance, auction venue, macro conditions and unsold lots?

No empirical causal estimates are asserted on this page.

Methodology, reproducibility & limitations

Calculation definitions

Royalty: piecewise cumulative statutory rates, £1,000 eligibility threshold and £12,500 cap. Seller proceeds = hammer price − seller commission − seller-allocated ARR. Buyer outlay = hammer price × (1 + buyer premium) + buyer-allocated ARR. Investor IRR solves the purchase and annual holding-cost cash flows against terminal seller proceeds. Real IRR = (1 + nominal IRR)/(1 + assumed inflation) − 1.

Historical comparison: pre-2024 EUR statutory schedule is applied to GBP sale price converted using assumed GBP/EUR, then converted back to GBP. No claim of historical FX accuracy. Break-even solves net proceeds = initial acquisition + cumulative nominal holding costs; it is not the zero-IRR cash-flow root when annual costs have different timing.

Eligibility and legal caveats

UK ARR applies to qualifying secondary-market sales involving an art-market professional, subject to qualifying rights and exemptions. A private sale without an art-market professional is generally excluded. Bought-as-stock treatment assumes acquisition directly from the artist, resale within three years and price no more than £10,000. Sales before 1 April 2024 require the historical euro-denominated rules and contract-date FX. Verify individual cases with collecting societies or legal advisers.
